# Data-free stochastic path sampling for the XY model

This repository implements and studies an XY adaptation of stochastic path sampling (SPS). The current experiments establish a small-system baseline and analyze its sampling limitations. They do **not** establish that generative sampling solves critical slowing down near the Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless (BKT) transition.

## Physical target and units

An `L x L` periodic lattice has one angle at each site, with each spin represented by `(cos(theta), sin(theta))`. The Hamiltonian counts each right and down nearest-neighbor bond once:

$$
H(\theta)=-J\sum_{x,y}\left[\cos(\theta_{x,y}-\theta_{x+1,y})+\cos(\theta_{x,y}-\theta_{x,y+1})\right].
$$

The desired equilibrium density is proportional to `exp(-beta * H)`. Published SPS experiments use `L=4`, `J=1`, `beta=1.12`, and `k_B=1`, corresponding to `T=1/beta ≈ 0.893`. There are 16 spins and 32 bonds. Plots report **energy per bond**, `e=H/(2L^2)`, rather than total energy or energy per site.

This temperature motivates a study near the large-system BKT region; a `4 x 4` lattice does not establish thermodynamic critical behavior.

## What “data-free” means here

Training does not read a precomputed dataset of equilibrium configurations. Instead, the model generates its own random paths and receives feedback from the known Hamiltonian and the probabilities of those paths. “Data-free” does not mean that training uses no samples: each update uses 64 newly generated paths.

Wolff cluster sampling has a separate role: it supplies an independently generated reference for validation. Its configurations do not enter the SPS training objective. The earlier supervised flow-matching notebooks used Wolff training data and are a historical baseline, not the current data-free method.

## Generating a candidate

The starting angles are independent uniform draws on `[-pi, pi)`. A forward neural drift and Gaussian noise rotate the spins over a path of fixed duration 1. For `N` steps, `h=1/N`, and one discrete update is

$$
\theta_{i+1}=\operatorname{wrap}\left[\theta_i+h f_F(\theta_i,t_i)+\sigma(t_i+h/2)\sqrt{h}\,\epsilon_i\right],\qquad \epsilon_i\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I).
$$

Here `wrap` returns angles to `[-pi, pi)`. Two small convolutional networks predict forward and backward angular drifts. Their inputs are cosine, sine, and a time channel; circular padding connects opposite lattice boundaries. A separate small network learns a scalar noise amplitude as a function of time. Neither DiT attention nor the historical `VorticityExtractor` is used in this SPS network. There has been no controlled comparison establishing that this network is better than DiT.

Transition probabilities use **wrapped Gaussian densities**: a sum over periodic images, rather than a Gaussian evaluated only at the shortest angular difference. The implementation evaluates nine images in float64, with a controlled truncation under its enforced standard-deviation bound. See [the implementation](../../src/sps_xy.py).

## Training from physics and path probabilities

For a complete path `z=(theta_0,...,theta_N)`, define

$$
Q_F(z)=p_0(\theta_0)\prod_{i=0}^{N-1}q_F(\theta_{i+1}\mid\theta_i),\qquad
Q_B(z\mid\theta_N)=\prod_{i=0}^{N-1}q_B(\theta_i\mid\theta_{i+1}).
$$

`p_0` is the known uniform starting density. The backward kernels are normalized conditional densities evaluated along the forward-generated path; training does not require a saved reference path run backward.

The log weight of a candidate path is

$$
\ell(z)=-\beta H(\theta_N)+\log Q_B(z\mid\theta_N)-\log Q_F(z).
$$

The training loss is the batch mean of `-ell/L^2`. It is a path-space reverse-KL objective up to an unknown additive constant: the extended target density is proportional to `exp(-beta*H(theta_N))*Q_B`. Normalization is unnecessary to calculate gradients. Energy favors physically likely endpoints, while the path-probability terms account for the distribution generated by the sampler; this is not simply minimizing endpoint energy.

Automatic differentiation through generated paths supplies gradients. Adam updates the forward drift, backward drift, and noise-network parameters. The baseline uses 32 training steps, batch size 64, and learning rate `0.001`. Training loss is an optimization diagnostic, **not** a test of correct equilibrium sampling.

## Correcting proposals with Metropolis-Hastings

After training, the model is fixed and proposes independent new paths. The independence Metropolis-Hastings (MH) decision is

$$
\alpha(z\to z')=\min\left(1,\exp[\ell(z')-\ell(z)]\right).
$$

This decision operates in the extended path space. Integrating the normalized backward kernels gives the desired equilibrium endpoint marginal. The implementation retains the endpoint and its old log weight, which are sufficient for later independent-proposal decisions. Rejection preserves **both**, and the repeated configuration remains in the Markov-chain record. Deleting repeated records would change the sampled distribution.

The correctness of the construction is distinct from whether a finite chain has equilibrated. A current path with unusually large relative weight can make replacement unlikely, even when new candidates have lower endpoint energy.

## Validation and cost

The study measures energy, vortex count, magnetization magnitude, and spatial correlations `G(1)` and `G(2)`. The vortex-pair statistic is `(number of +1 plaquettes + number of -1 plaquettes)/2`; it is a counting convention, not a geometric pairing of defects. At this lattice size, `G(1)` is redundant with the energy.

The finite-run screening checks per-chain effective sample size (ESS), classical split R-hat, and agreement with Wolff within three combined standard errors. These are diagnostics for selected observables, not a proof that the full distribution has mixed. Errors and ESS estimates require sufficient stationarity.

Acceptance, chain ESS, proposal importance ESS, and ESS per second answer different questions. Published ESS per second includes proposal generation, device-to-host transfer, initialization, burn-in, and MH decisions; it excludes training, plotting, and other diagnostic work. Training cost is recorded separately. There is no matched total-cost comparison demonstrating a speedup over Wolff.

## Method sources and available evidence

The SPS framework is credited to Chen et al., [*Stochastic Path Sampler For Lattice Field Theory*, arXiv:2606.13790v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13790). This project adapts the framework to periodic XY angles; it is not an exact reproduction of the paper's phi-four experiments. Wolff references use [Wolff's cluster algorithm](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.62.361). Bibliographic entries are in [references.bib](../../literature/references.bib).

The GitHub repository provides code, configurations, selected figures, and compact result summaries. Original checkpoint files, raw sample banks, full intermediate paths, and downloaded job archives are not supplied here. In fact, complete intermediate paths were not saved during these runs. Saved-weight MH replay and endpoint audits therefore do not independently reconstruct every path density. See [the results and their limits](results.md).
